RESOLUCIÓN CONJUNTA
Para conmemorar el Semiquincentenario de los Estados Unidos de América y solicitar al
Presidente y al Congreso de los Estados Unidos la admisión de Puerto Rico como
Estado de la Unión; para requerir que copia de esta Resolución Conjunta sea remitida
al Presidente y al Congreso de los Estados Unidos de América; y para otros fines
relacionados.
EXPOSICIÓN DE MOTIVOS
Desde el 4 de julio de 2026, Estados Unidos de América conmemoró el 250
Aniversario de su Declaración de Independencia: carta fundacional del compromiso de
nuestra Nación con el gobierno por consentimiento de los gobernados y la creencia de que
todos los hombres son creados iguales, que están dotados por su Creador de ciertos
Derechos inalienables, que entre estos están la Vida, la Libertad y la búsqueda de la
Felicidad.
Los puertorriqueños somos ciudadanos de los Estados Unidos desde 1917 y hemos
contribuido continuamente a la vida económica, militar, cultural y cívica de nuestra
Nación.

2
Sin embargo, en 2026 más de tres millones de ciudadanos estadounidenses residen
en Puerto Rico bajo un estatus territorial sujeto a la autoridad plena del Congreso bajo el
Artículo IV, Sección 3, Cláusula 2 de la Constitución de los Estados Unidos, y el Congreso
posee la autoridad bajo el Artículo IV, Sección 3, Cláusula 1 para admitir nuevos Estados
a la Unión.
La Corte Suprema de los Estados Unidos ha reconocido repetidamente que Puerto
Rico sigue siendo un territorio sujeto a la Cláusula Territorial de la Constitución, y que
cualquier cambio permanente en el estatus político de Puerto Rico requiere acción del
Congreso.
El pueblo de Puerto Rico ha ejercido reiteradamente su derecho a determinar su
futuro político a través de plebiscitos y ha expresado su preferencia por que Puerto Rico
se convierta en un Estado de la Unión. En un plebiscito celebrado el 6 de noviembre de
2012, el 53.97 por ciento de los votantes participantes rechazó la continuación del estatus
territorial existente. En el plebiscito celebrado el 5 de noviembre de 2024, la estadidad
recibió el 58.61 por ciento de los votos legales emitidos entre las opciones de estatus
presentadas al electorado.
En todos los plebiscitos realizados durante la última década, incluidos los celebrados
en 2012, 2017, 2020 y 2024, la estadidad recibió la mayoría de los votos legales emitidos
entre las opciones de estatus presentadas al electorado.
La existencia continua del estatus territorial deja a los ciudadanos estadounidenses
que residen en Puerto Rico sin representación con derecho a voto en el Congreso y sin
participación en la elección del Presidente y Vicepresidente de los Estados Unidos de
América.
No obstante, generaciones de puertorriqueños han demostrado su patriotismo a
través de un servicio distinguido y sacrificio en las Fuerzas Armadas de los Estados
Unidos, ganándose los más altos honores y reconocimientos militares de nuestra Nación,

3
incluso cuando los ciudadanos estadounidenses que residen en Puerto Rico no pueden
votar por el Comandante en Jefe bajo el cual sirven.
Todo lo anterior nos lleva a concluir que en el marco de la conmemoración del
Semiquincentenario de la fundación de la Nación, representa una oportunidad histórica
para que Estados Unidos reafirme su compromiso con el principio de que el gobierno
deriva sus poderes legítimos del consentimiento de los gobernados, tal como se refleja en
la admisión de treinta y dos antiguos territorios estadounidenses que solicitaron al
Congreso su incorporación como estados —basándose en el consentimiento expresado
democráticamente por sus habitantes— y fueron debidamente admitidos como estados de
la Unión. Ninguno de ellos fue rechazado, Puerto Rico no debe ser la excepción.

JOINT RESOLUTION
To commemorate the 250th Anniversary of the United States of America and to petition the
President and the Congress of the United States for the admission of Puerto Rico as a
State of the Union; to require that a copy of this Joint Resolution be transmitted to the
President and the Congress of the United States of America; and for other related
purposes.
MOTIVES EXPOSITION
On July 4, 2026, the United States of America commemorated the 250th anniversary of its
Declaration of Independence—the founding document of our Nation’s commitment to government
by the consent of the governed and the belief that all men are created equal, that they are endowed
by their Creator with certain unalienable Rights, and that among these are Life, Liberty, and the
pursuit of Happiness.
Puerto Ricans have been citizens of the United States since 1917 and have continuously
contributed to the economic, military, cultural, and civic life of our Nation.
However, in 2026, more than three million U.S. citizens reside in Puerto Rico under a
territorial status subject to the plenary authority of Congress under Article IV, Section 3, Clause 2
of the United States Constitution, and Congress possesses the authority under Article IV, Section 3,
Clause 1 to admit new states to the Union.
The United States Supreme Court has repeatedly recognized that Puerto Rico remains a
territory subject to the Territorial Clause of the Constitution, and that any permanent change to
Puerto Rico's political status requires action by Congress.
The Constitution of Puerto Rico, approved by Congress under Federal Law 82-447 and
subsequently amended, establishes a republican form of local government compatible with the
United States Constitution and reflects the democratic traditions and institutions shared by the
people of Puerto Rico and the various States of the Union.
The people of Puerto Rico have repeatedly exercised their right to determine their political
future through plebiscites and have expressed a preference for Puerto Rico to become a State of the
Union. In a plebiscite held on November 6, 2012, 53.97 percent of participating voters rejected the
continuation of the existing territorial status. In the plebiscite held on November 5, 2024, statehood
received 58.61 percent of the legal votes cast among the status options presented to the electorate.

7
In all plebiscites held over the last decade—including those in 2012, 2017, 2020, and 2024—
statehood received a majority of the legal votes cast among the status options presented to the
electorate.
The continued existence of the current territorial status leaves U.S. citizens residing in Puerto
Rico without voting representation in Congress and without a voice in the election of the President
and Vice President of the United States of America.
Yet, generations of Puerto Ricans have demonstrated their patriotism through distinguished
service and sacrifice in the United States Armed Forces, earning our Nation’s highest military
honors and recognitions—even though U.S. citizens residing in Puerto Rico cannot vote for the
Commander-in-Chief under whom they serve.
All of this leads us to conclude that the commemoration of the Nation’s Semi quincentennial
presents a historic opportunity for the United States to reaffirm its commitment to the principle that
government derives its legitimate powers from the consent of the governed—a principle reflected
in the admission of thirty-two former U.S. territories that petitioned Congress for statehood, based
on the democratically expressed consent of their inhabitants, and were duly admitted as states of the
Union. None of them were rejected; Puerto Rico should not be the exception.
BE IT RESOLVED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
1 Section 1.—Commemoration of our Nation’s Semi quincentennial.
2 The Legislative Assembly and the Governor of Puerto Rico, acting on behalf of the People
3 of Puerto Rico, reaffirm their commitment to the United States Constitution, democratic
4 self-government, and the principles articulated in the Declaration of Independence.
5 Therefore, consistent with the principles of representative government embodied in the
6 Declaration of Independence, the year of our Nation’s Semi quincentennial is the
7 appropriate and historic occasion for the United States to strengthen the Union by allowing
8 the admission of Puerto Rico as a State.
9 Section 2.- Petition for Statehood.

8
1 The Legislative Assembly and the Governor of Puerto Rico formally request that
2 Congress, pursuant to its authority under Article IV, Section 3, Clause 1 of the United
3 States Constitution, enact legislation providing for the admission of Puerto Rico as a State
4 of the Union on an equal footing with the several States.
5 Section 3.—Presidential public policy.
6 The Legislative Assembly and the Governor of Puerto Rico respectfully request the
7 President of the United States to support and work with Congress toward the enactment
8 and implementation of legislation and all other measures necessary to achieve the
9 admission of Puerto Rico as a State.
10 Section 4.– Public policy of the Government of Puerto Rico.
11 The Legislative Assembly and the Governor of Puerto Rico declare that shared
12 American values, economic capacity, military service, importance to national security, and
13 Puerto Rico’s historical integration into the constitutional, economic, and political life of
14 our Nation demonstrate its readiness to be admitted as a State of the Union. Therefore, it
15 is declared to be the public policy of the Government of Puerto Rico to formally petition
16 Puerto Rico’s admission as a State of the Union in order to achieve full equal rights and
17 responsibilities for the more than three million citizens of the United States residing in
18 Puerto Rico.
19 Section 5.– Conclusion of territorial status.
20 The Legislative Assembly and the Governor of Puerto Rico declare that no enduring
21 territorial status can satisfy the principles of equal citizenship and government by consent

1 that inspired the American Revolution and continue to guide the constitutional
2 development of the United States.
3 Section 6. Resources of the Government of Puerto Rico.
4 Given that statehood is the public policy of our government, the use of all
5 government resources is authorized and mandated to—without neglecting the needs of
6 our people—publicize this Joint Resolution and undertake all necessary efforts in any
7 available forum to fulfill and enforce its purposes.
8 Section 7.—Translation and transmission.
9 Copies of this Joint Resolution shall be transmitted to the President of the United States,
10 the President of the United States Senate, the Speaker of the United States House of
11 Representatives, and each member of Congress, as a declaration that the People of Puerto
12 Rico seek the fulfillment of our Nation’s foundational promise that government derives its
13 legitimate powers from the consent of the governed and that all citizens are entitled to
14 equal rights, representation, and responsibilities under the Constitution.
15 Section 8.—Bilingual Puerto Rico.
